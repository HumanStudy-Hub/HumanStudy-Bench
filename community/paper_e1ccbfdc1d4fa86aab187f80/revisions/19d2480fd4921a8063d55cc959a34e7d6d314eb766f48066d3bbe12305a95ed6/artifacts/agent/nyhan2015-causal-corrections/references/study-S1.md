# Swensen resignation dispatches experiment (YouGov survey, Oct 13-16, 2012)

`S1`

A four-condition, block-randomized online survey experiment in which respondents read dispatches about a fictional Alaska state senator's resignation (control, innuendo, denial, causal correction) and then rated his favorability and the likelihood he took bribes / resigned because of an investigation. The paper reports this one experiment.

## Hypotheses

### Hypothesis: causal correction displaces misinformation

`h-main`

- **Statement**: False information may continue to influence beliefs and attitudes even after being debunked if it is not replaced by an alternate causal explanation; a causal explanation for an unexplained event should therefore be more effective than a denial, even a denial backed by strong evidence. (p. 1)
- **Scope**: A single fictional breaking-news event (a politician's sudden resignation) in which the innuendo is never definitively disproved. (p. 5, p. 3)

## Participants

### Participants: YouGov respondents

`n-part`

- **Setting**: Online survey fielded by YouGov, October 13-16, 2012, as part of the 2012 Cooperative Campaign Analysis Project; a single online session per respondent. (p. 3, sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Roles**: Respondent in one of four conditions; no confederates or other roles are described. (p. 4)
- **Recruitment**: Respondents were matched and weighted to population targets using YouGov's sample matching procedure, designed to approximate a nationally representative sample. (p. 4)
- **Eligibility and exclusion criteria**: Not stated in the paper. (p. 4)
- **Final sample**: 1000 respondents in the final sample. (p. 4)
- **Completed**: Data were provided by YouGov for 1000 respondents who completed the study; no known attrition. (sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Analysed**: Models 1-2 (favorability) N = 987; Models 3-4 (bribes) N = 986; Models 5-6 (investigation) N = 764. Listwise deletion removed 13, 14 and 5 cases respectively; the investigation question was not asked of 231 control respondents. (p. 15, sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Characteristics**: Approximately representative of the U.S. population; 35% identified as Democrats and 27% as Republicans. Unweighted gender, education, race/ethnicity and age by condition are in the appendix balance table. (p. 4, p. 21)
- **Number of respondents per condition**: Not stated in the paper. (p. 15)
- **Sampling unit**: Individual respondent (individual-level randomization and analysis). (p. 4)
- **Compensation**: YouGov's normal survey participation incentives; no additional incentives. (sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Attrition**: No known attrition. (sources/supp_materials.txt)

## Design

### Design: four-condition block-randomized survey experiment

`d-design`

- **Type**: Between-subjects; four conditions that differ in the number and content of dispatches read; one dispatch per screen. (p. 4)
- **Control**: Control condition: resignation with no indication why he resigned. (p. 5)
- **Assignment**: Respondents were randomly assigned, block-randomized on their answer to an ANES question about how many people running the government are crooked, to improve precision and balance prior beliefs about official misconduct. Assignment was generated by the YouGov survey platform. (p. 4, sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Blocks [derived, to check]**: Blocks are taken to be the four response options of the pre-treatment 'crooked' question. (p. 4, p. 19)
  - how it was derived: The paper says respondents were block-randomized on their response to this question; the appendix lists four response options; the paper does not state the blocks explicitly.
- **Order**: Dispatches were read one per page in chronological order, moving downward in Table 1, within each respondent's condition; the pre-treatment question comes first, the delay tasks then the outcome questions come after the dispatches. (p. 4, p. 14, p. 19)
- **Blinding**: Subjects were blind to which condition they were in. (sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Manipulation check**: None reported as such. A three-question recall battery was used to check that effects were not due to differing recall of the event. (p. 7)
- **Pre-specification**: All outcomes and conditions were specified before the study (only DVs and experimental conditions tested); no registration reported. (sources/supp_materials.txt)

## Analysis

### OLS models of treatment effects (Table 2)

`a-ols`

- **Aim**: Estimate the effect of innuendo, denial and causal correction on favorability, belief in bribery/illegal practices and belief in the investigation rumor. (p. 6)
- **Inputs**: Condition indicators; DVs favorability, bribes, investigation. (p. 6)
- **Method**: OLS with condition indicators. Models 1-4 use the control condition as reference; Models 5-6 (investigation) use the innuendo condition as reference and exclude control. Each DV is estimated with inverse probability weights (odd models) and YouGov survey weights (even models). (p. 6, p. 15)
- **Criterion**: Significance flagged at p<.01 and p<.05; standard errors in parentheses. (p. 15)
- **Robustness**: Ordered probit gives substantively identical results; unweighted OLS gives substantively identical results. (p. 6)
- **Standard error type (classical or robust)**: Not stated in the paper. (p. 15)

### Pairwise comparisons between conditions

`a-pairwise`

- **Aim**: Compare every pair of conditions for each DV. (p. 24)
- **Method**: Pairwise differences computed from the inverse probability weight models in Table 2. (p. 24)

### Robustness: ordered probit and unweighted OLS

`a-robust`

- **Aim**: Check that the results do not depend on OLS or on weighting. (p. 6)
- **Method**: Ordered probit with IPW or survey weights; OLS without weights. (p. 22, p. 23)

### Recall check

`a-recall`

- **Aim**: Test whether recall of the event differs between the innuendo, denial and causal conditions. (p. 7)
- **Method**: OLS of recall score on denial and causal indicators with the innuendo condition as reference (control was not asked all recall questions), IPW and survey weights. (p. 25)

### Randomization balance check

`a-balance`

- **Aim**: Check that demographic distributions do not differ across conditions beyond chance. (p. 4)
- **Method**: Unweighted and survey-weighted tests of demographic distributions; tests are not possible with inverse probability weights. (p. 21)

## Reported results

### Reported: favorability (Models 1-2)

`res-fav`

- **Result kind**: reported (p. 6)
- **Descriptives**: Condition means from the IPW model: control 2.75, innuendo 2.17, denial 2.36, causal 2.83. (p. 24)
- **Effect**: Relative to control (IPW; survey): innuendo -0.58, -0.52; denial -0.39, -0.41; causal 0.08, -0.20. Causal vs innuendo: 0.66 (Model 1), 0.32 (Model 2); causal vs denial 0.46 (Model 1). (p. 15, p. 6, p. 24)
- **Uncertainty**: SEs (IPW; survey): innuendo 0.10, 0.17; denial 0.10, 0.15; causal 0.10, 0.16; constant 2.75 and 2.86; N = 987. (p. 15)
- **Test**: Innuendo and denial significantly lower than control (p<.01); causal not different from control; causal significantly above innuendo (p<.01 IPW, p<.05 survey); denial vs innuendo p<.10 only with IPW; causal vs denial p<.01 IPW, p<.12 survey. (p. 6)

### Reported: belief in bribery or illegal practices (Models 3-4)

`res-bribes`

- **Result kind**: reported (p. 7)
- **Descriptives**: Condition means from the IPW model: control 2.86, innuendo 3.65, denial 3.41, causal 2.93. (p. 24)
- **Effect**: Relative to control (IPW; survey): innuendo 0.78, 0.67; denial 0.55, 0.52; causal 0.07, 0.16. Causal vs innuendo -0.72 (Model 3), -0.51 (Model 4); causal vs denial -0.48 (Model 3), -0.36 (Model 4); denial vs innuendo -0.23. (p. 15, p. 7, p. 24)
- **Uncertainty**: SEs (IPW; survey): innuendo 0.10, 0.16; denial 0.10, 0.15; causal 0.10, 0.15; constant 2.86 and 2.94; N = 986. (p. 15)
- **Test**: Innuendo and denial significantly above control (p<.01); causal not different from control; causal significantly below innuendo (p<.01) and denial (p<.01 IPW, p<.05 survey); denial vs innuendo p<.05 with IPW but not with survey weights. (p. 7)

### Reported: belief he resigned because of an investigation (Models 5-6)

`res-inv`

- **Result kind**: reported (p. 7, p. 8)
- **Descriptives**: Condition means from the IPW model: innuendo 3.46, denial 3.21, causal 2.87. (p. 24)

## Simulation scope

### Simulation scope

`sim-1`

- **Steps [implementation, to check]**: Possible: pre-treatment question, scenario introduction, the condition's dispatches one at a time, and the outcome questions, all as printed. Excluded: the unscramble and word search delay tasks (content not printed) and the recall questions (position in the survey not stated). (p. 4, p. 5)
- **Roles [implementation, to check]**: Every original participant is an AI participant; the paper has no confederates or scripted roles. Dispatch text is pre-written stimulus, shown as printed. (p. 4)
- **Conditions [implementation, to check]**: All four conditions can be run with the printed dispatches. Block randomization on the pre-treatment question is replaced by running an equal number of AI participants per condition; survey weights and inverse probability weights cannot be reproduced. (p. 4, p. 6)
- **Outcomes [implementation, to check]**: Favorability, belief in bribery, and (outside control) belief in the investigation rumor can be collected on the printed scales and compared with Table 2 and the appendix means. Recall is not collected. (p. 5, p. 6)
- **Limits [implementation, to check]**: An AI participant has no working-memory interference, no real news habits and no demographic or political background unless one is added; results are a simulation, not observations of people, and agreement with the paper would not show that people behave the same way. The survey weights, sample demographics and panel are not reproduced. (p. 4)

## Open issues

- **Delay task content is not given** [decision, `i-delay`, S1]: The paper says respondents completed unscramble and word search tasks to clear working memory; the appendix shows only a placeholder for the delay content. Neither task items nor duration are stated. Suggested: Supply the task content and duration if known; otherwise keep the replay without a delay and report it as an adaptation. (p. 5, p. 19)
- **Position and audience of the recall questions are not stated** [check, `i-recall-pos`, S1]: The recall questions are in the appendix and the footnote says a three-question battery was used, but the paper does not say where in the survey they came, and the control group was not asked the second and third questions. Suggested: Obtain the survey flow from the authors. The replay does not administer recall. (p. 7, p. 25)
- **Eligibility criteria are not reported** [check, `i-eligibility`, n-part]: The paper describes the panel but gives no eligibility or exclusion rules (the supplement's reporting checklist points to pages 2-3, which do not list them). Suggested: Ask the authors or YouGov for the eligibility rules. (p. 4)
- **Respondents per condition are not reported** [check, `i-cells`, n-part]: Only model Ns (987, 986, 764) and the 231 control respondents not asked the investigation question can be inferred; the sizes of the innuendo, denial and causal arms are not given. Suggested: Request cell sizes from the authors or the replication data. (p. 15, sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **Standard error type is not stated** [check, `i-sevariance`, a-ols]: Table 2 reports standard errors in parentheses but does not say whether they are classical or robust. Suggested: Check the replication data when posted. (p. 15)
- **Block definition is inferred** [check, `i-blocks`, d-design]: The paper says respondents were block-randomized on their answer to the crooked question but does not list the blocks. Suggested: Confirm with the authors. (p. 4, p. 19)
- **Weights cannot be reproduced** [decision, `i-weights`, v-weights]: YouGov survey weights and the block inverse probability weights depend on the panel and the realized assignment, which are not available. Suggested: Decide whether to treat unweighted OLS as the comparison model (default). (p. 6)
- **AI participant background is not specified** [decision, `i-persona`, sim-1]: The sample is described as approximately representative of the U.S. population with 35% Democrats and 27% Republicans, but the paper reports no individual-level characteristics that a prompt could use. Suggested: Decide whether AI participants should be given demographic or partisan backgrounds, and label that choice as a researcher decision. (p. 4)
- **Sample demographics are available only on request** [check, `i-demo`, n-part]: Details of the sample's demographic make-up are said to be available upon request; only party identification percentages and unweighted balance tables are printed. Suggested: Request the details from the authors. (p. 4, p. 21)
- **Response rate and participant flow are not reported** [check, `i-flow`, n-part]: The supplement's reporting checklist lists the response rate and CONSORT diagram as pending with YouGov. Suggested: Request from the authors. (sources/supp_materials.txt)
- **The senator is called Don Swensen in Table 1 and Dan Swensen in the appendix questions** [check, `i-name`, m-q-fav]: Table 1 uses 'Don Swensen' while the appendix outcome and recall questions say 'Dan Swensen'. Suggested: Confirm with the authors which spelling respondents saw in the questions. (p. 19, p. 13)
- **Order of the outcome questions differs between the text and the appendix** [check, `i-qorder`, S1]: The text introduces favorability, then bribes, then the investigation question; the appendix shows favorability, investigation, then bribes, and puts the investigation question on its own page. Suggested: Confirm the order with the authors. (p. 5, p. 20)
- **Numeric codes in the appendix response options** [check, `i-codes`, m-q-fav]: The appendix prints bracketed numbers after each option (e.g. 'Very favorable [6]'); it is not stated whether respondents saw them or they are coding only. Suggested: Confirm with the authors. (p. 19, p. 20)
