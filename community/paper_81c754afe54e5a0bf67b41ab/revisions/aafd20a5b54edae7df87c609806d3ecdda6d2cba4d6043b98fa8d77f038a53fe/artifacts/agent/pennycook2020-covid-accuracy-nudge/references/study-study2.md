# Study 2: accuracy-nudge experiment on sharing intentions

`study2`

856 analysed U.S. Lucid participants shared (6-point scale) the same 30 headlines after a control start or after rating one unrelated headline.

## Hypotheses

### Study 2 hypothesis: an accuracy nudge raises sharing discernment

`h_s2`

- **Statement [derived]**: Subtly making the concept of accuracy salient (rating one unrelated headline first) increases the veracity of the COVID-19 headlines people say they would share. (p. 2, p. 7)
  - how it was derived: The paper reports 'As predicted' for the veracity-by-treatment interaction.
- **Scope [derived]**: Sharing intentions for the 30 Study 1 headlines; control vs. treatment between participants. (p. 6)
  - how it was derived: From the Study 2 method.

## Participants

### Study 2 participants (Lucid, U.S.)

`p_s2`

- **Setting**: Online survey; Lucid respondents; run from March 13 to March 15, 2020. (p. 6)
- **Roles [derived]**: Participants only; no scripted roles. (p. 6)
  - how it was derived: No other role appears in the Study 2 method.
- **Recruitment**: Following the same sample-size considerations as in Study 1, we recruited 1,000 participants using Lucid. (p. 6)
- **Eligibility [derived]**: Participants had to indicate using Facebook or Twitter; 177 who did not did not complete the survey. (p. 6)
  - how it was derived: Same screening statement as Study 1.
- **Began the study**: 1,145 (p. 6)
- **Completed (retained)**: 856 (177 non-users and a further 112 who did not complete were removed). (p. 6)
- **Analysed**: 856 participants (25,627 ratings, Supplement Table S5); all participants kept regardless of attentiveness. (p. 6, p. 7)
- **Participants per condition**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Characteristics**: Mean age = 47 years, age range = 18–86; 385 men, 463 women, and 8 who responded 'other/prefer not to answer'. (p. 6)
- **Sampling unit [derived]**: Participant (each rated 30 headlines). (p. 6, p. 7)
  - how it was derived: Ratings analysed at the rating level clustered on participant and headline.
- **Compensation**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Attrition**: 177 did not indicate using Facebook or Twitter; 112 did not complete. (p. 6)

### Study 2 design

`d_s2`

- **Type [derived]**: Between-participants (control vs. treatment) with headline veracity varying within participants. (p. 6, p. 3)

- **Assignment** (verbatim, p. 6):

- **Manipulation check [derived]**: None reported; the manipulation is described as subtle and not explicitly linked to the main task. (p. 6)

### Study 2 control condition

`cond_s2_control`

- **Definition**: They began the news-sharing task as in Study 1. (p. 6)

### Study 2 treatment condition (accuracy nudge)

`cond_s2_treatment`

- **Definition**: They rated the accuracy of a single headline (unrelated to COVID-19) before beginning the news-sharing task, framed as being for a pretest. (p. 6)

## Procedure

Order of steps:

- Treatment: rate the accuracy of one unrelated headline (framed as a pretest) (participant sees: Treatment pretest headline) (p. 6, sources/study2_materials.txt)
  - conditions: Study 2 treatment condition (accuracy nudge)
- Repeated: Rate sharing likelihood for 30 headlines (random order) (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 2 sharing question) (p. 6)
  - Rate how likely to share one headline (1-6) (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 2 sharing question) (p. 6)
- Debrief: true headlines re-presented (participant sees: Debriefing) (p. 3)
- Additional measures from Study 1 (CRT, science quiz, MMS, demographics) (participant sees: Individual-differences instruments (CRT, science knowledge, MMS, demographics)) (p. 7)
  - **Order relative to the sharing task**: Not stated in the paper. (p. 7)
- (back to: Debrief: true headlines re-presented)
- Three attention screeners (participant sees: Attention screener questions) (p. 7)

### Study 2 procedure

`proc_s2`

- **Task**: Participants indicated how likely they would be to share each of the same 30 headlines (Study 1 set). (p. 6)
- **Goal [derived]**: Test whether making accuracy salient first increases sharing discernment. (p. 2)
  - how it was derived: From the Study 2 aim.
- **Actions [derived]**: Treatment only: rate one unrelated headline as a pretest. Then all: 30-headline sharing task; the additional measures of Study 1; three screeners; debriefing. (p. 6, p. 7, p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Combined from the Study 2 method passages.
- **What participants saw [derived]**: One headline post at a time with the sharing question; treatment participants had first seen the pretest headline. (p. 6)
  - how it was derived: No social information is described.
- **Feedback**: Debriefing at the end: the true headlines were re-presented. (p. 3)

## Analysis

### Study 2 main analysis: veracity × treatment

`a_s2_main`

- **Aim**: Test whether the treatment increases sharing discernment. (p. 7)
- **Inputs**: Sharing intentions rescaled to 0-1; veracity coded false = 0, true = 1; condition coded control = 0, treatment = 1. (p. 7, p. 6)
- **Method**: Linear regression at the level of the rating with robust standard errors clustered on participants and headline. (p. 7, p. 10)
- **Criterion [derived]**: Positive veracity × treatment interaction. (p. 7)
  - how it was derived: The prediction is tested via this interaction.
- **Robustness**: Excluding headlines without clear claims of fact or that were political (Supplement Table S6); attentiveness thresholds (Table S5); crossed random effects (Table S7). (p. 7, p. 6, p. 8, p. 9)

### Study 2 moderation by individual differences

`a_s2_mod`

- **Aim**: Test whether the treatment effect on sharing discernment depends on CRT, science knowledge, partisanship, distance to the epicenter or MMS score. (p. 7)
- **Method**: Three-way interactions among headline veracity, treatment and each individual-differences measure. (p. 7)

## Reported results

### Study 2: treatment increased sharing discernment

`res_s2_main`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 7)
- **Interaction**: β = 0.039, F(1, 25623) = 17.88, p < .0001 (veracity × treatment). (p. 7)
- **Simple effects of veracity**: Control: d = 0.050, 95% CI [−0.033, 0.133], F(1, 25623) = 1.41, p = .24. Treatment: d = 0.142, 95% CI [0.049, 0.235], F(1, 25623) = 8.89, p = .003. (p. 7)
- **Ratio**: Sharing discernment was 2.8 times higher in the treatment condition than in the control condition. (p. 7)

### Study 2: no significant moderation of the treatment effect

`res_s2_mod`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 7)
- **Test**: The treatment effect on sharing discernment was not significantly moderated by CRT performance, science knowledge, partisanship, distance to the nearest epicenter, or MMS score (all three-way interactions p > .10). (p. 7)

### Study 2 robustness tables (Supplement S5-S7)

`res_s2_supp`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 6)
- **Veracity × condition across robustness checks**: Attentiveness thresholds (all, ≥1, ≥2, all 3 screeners): 0.0343, 0.0330, 0.0390 (p<.001) and 0.0320 (not significant). Headline exclusions (7 models): 0.0343, 0.0322, 0.0305, 0.0306, 0.0327, 0.0304, 0.0299 (all p<.001). Crossed random effects: 3.430e-02, SE 1.112e-02, t = 3.085, p = 0.00238. (p. 6, p. 8, p. 9)

## Simulation scope

### Study 2 simulation scope

`sim_s2`

- **Steps [implementation]**: Replay the 30-headline sharing task. Treatment adds the pretest headline first, which cannot be replayed until its headline, question and framing wording are supplied. Other measures are not replayed. (p. 6)
- **Roles [implementation]**: Every participant is an AI participant; no scripted roles. (p. 6)
- **Conditions [implementation]**: Control is runnable; treatment is blocked by the missing pretest materials. (p. 6)
- **Outcomes [implementation]**: 6-point sharing ratings, rescaled to 0-1, sharing discernment and the veracity × treatment interaction (only when both arms exist). (p. 7)
- **Limits [implementation]**: Text-only headlines; one prompt per participant containing all 30 headlines; subtle manipulation may behave differently for a language model; hypothetical sharing; no demographic persona; AI agreement with the paper would not show that people behave the same way. (p. 3, p. 9)
