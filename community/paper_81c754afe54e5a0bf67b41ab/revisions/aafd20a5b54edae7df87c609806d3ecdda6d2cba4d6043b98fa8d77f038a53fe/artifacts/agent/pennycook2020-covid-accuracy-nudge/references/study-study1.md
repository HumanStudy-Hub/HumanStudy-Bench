# Study 1: accuracy judgments vs. sharing intentions (between participants)

`study1`

853 analysed U.S. Lucid participants judged either accuracy or sharing of 30 COVID-19 headlines; individual-differences correlates.

## Hypotheses

### Study 1 hypothesis: sharing is less truth-discerning than accuracy judgments

`h_s1`

- **Statement [derived]**: Discernment between true and false COVID-19 headlines is higher when participants judge accuracy than when they decide about sharing (a veracity-by-condition interaction); discernment is also related to analytic cognitive style and science knowledge. (p. 2, p. 4)
  - how it was derived: The paper reports 'As predicted' for the interaction and lists the correlational aims; the statement merges the two passages.
- **Scope [derived]**: True and false COVID-19 news headlines; U.S. adults on Lucid; March 2020. (p. 2, p. 3)
  - how it was derived: From the Study 1 aim and sample description.

## Participants

### Study 1 participants (Lucid, U.S.)

`p_s1`

- **Setting**: Online survey; Lucid respondents; run on March 12, 2020. (p. 3)
- **Roles [derived]**: Participants only; no confederates or scripted partners are described. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: No other role appears in the Study 1 method.
- **Recruitment**: We recruited 1,000 participants using Lucid, an online recruiting source that aggregates survey respondents from many respondent providers; quota sampling matched to the U.S. public on age, gender, ethnicity, and geographic region. (p. 3)
- **Eligibility [derived]**: Participants had to indicate using Facebook or Twitter; 192 who did not were not given the survey. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: The paper says these 192 'did not complete the survey' because they did not indicate using Facebook or Twitter.
- **Began the study**: 1,143 (p. 3)
- **Completed (retained)**: 853 (192 non-users of Facebook/Twitter and a further 98 who did not finish were removed). (p. 3)
- **Analysed**: 853 participants (25,590 ratings: 853 × 30 headlines, Supplement Table S2); all participants kept regardless of attentiveness. (p. 3, p. 4)
- **Participants per condition**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Characteristics**: Mean age = 46 years, age range = 18–90; 357 men, 482 women, and 14 who responded 'other/prefer not to answer'. (p. 3)
- **Sampling unit [derived]**: Participant (each rated or considered sharing 30 headlines). (p. 3, p. 4)
  - how it was derived: Ratings are analysed at the level of the rating, clustered on participant and headline.
- **Compensation**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Attrition**: 192 did not indicate using Facebook or Twitter; 98 did not finish and were removed. (p. 3)

## Design

### Study 1 design

`d_s1`

- **Type [derived]**: Between-participants comparison of two question types (accuracy vs. sharing) with headline veracity (false vs. true) varying within participants. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Combined from the condition description and the 15 false / 15 true headline set.
- **Control [derived]**: No separate control: the accuracy condition is the comparison for the sharing condition. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Both conditions are described as experimental conditions.
- **Assignment** (verbatim, p. 3):
  > Each participant was randomly assigned to one of two conditions.
- **Order**: Headlines were presented in a random order; the order of the yes/no options was counterbalanced (no/yes vs. yes/no) across participants. (p. 3)

## Conditions

### Study 1 accuracy condition

`cond_s1_accuracy`

- **Definition**: Participants were asked, 'To the best of your knowledge, is the claim in the above headline accurate?' (yes/no). (p. 3)

### Study 1 sharing condition

`cond_s1_sharing`

- **Definition**: Participants were asked, 'Would you consider sharing this story online (for example, through Facebook or Twitter?)' (yes/no). (p. 3)

## Procedure

Order of steps:

- Answer two COVID-19 questions (participant sees: Two COVID-19 questions before the news task) (p. 3)
  - when accuracy condition:
    - Repeated: Judge accuracy of 30 headlines (random order) (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 1 accuracy question) (p. 3)
      - conditions: Study 1 accuracy condition
      - Rate one headline: accurate? yes/no (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 1 accuracy question) (p. 3)
        - conditions: Study 1 accuracy condition
    - Debrief: true headlines re-presented (participant sees: Debriefing) (p. 3)
  - when sharing condition:
    - Repeated: Decide on sharing 30 headlines (random order) (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 1 sharing question) (p. 3)
      - conditions: Study 1 sharing condition
      - Answer for one headline: would you consider sharing? yes/no (participant sees: COVID-19 news headlines (15 false, 15 true); Study 1 sharing question) (p. 3)
        - conditions: Study 1 sharing condition
    - (back to: Debrief: true headlines re-presented)
- Complete CRT, science-knowledge quiz, MMS and demographics (participant sees: Individual-differences instruments (CRT, science knowledge, MMS, demographics)) (p. 3, p. 4)
  - **Order relative to the news task**: Not stated in the paper. (p. 4)
- (back to: Debrief: true headlines re-presented)
- Three attention screeners at separate points (participant sees: Attention screener questions) (p. 4)
  - **Placement** (verbatim, p. 4):
    > Just prior to the news-evaluation and news-sharing tasks, after the CRT, and after the science-knowledge scale and MMS.

### Study 1 procedure

`proc_s1`

- **Task [derived]**: Participants viewed 30 COVID-19 headlines (Facebook-post format) in random order and answered a yes/no question about each, either accuracy or sharing depending on condition. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Combined from the conditions, format and order passages.
- **Actions [derived]**: Two COVID-19 questions; news task; CRT; science-knowledge quiz; MMS; demographics; three attention screeners. (p. 3, p. 4)
  - how it was derived: Listed from the Methods; the order of the individual-differences measures relative to the news task is not stated.
- **What participants saw [derived]**: One headline post at a time with the condition's question; no ratings or behaviour of other participants. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: The paper describes no group or social information.
- **Feedback**: Debriefing at the end: the true headlines were re-presented. (p. 3)

## Variables

### Headline veracity

`v_veracity`

- **Role [derived]**: iv (p. 4)
  - how it was derived: Coded -0.5 false / 0.5 true in Study 1 and 0 / 1 in Study 2.
- **Type [derived]**: binary, within participants (p. 4, p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Every participant sees 15 false and 15 true headlines.
- **Observation unit [derived]**: headline (p. 4)
  - how it was derived: Analysed at the level of the rating.

### Study 1 condition (accuracy vs. sharing)

`v_cond_s1`

- **Role [derived]**: iv (p. 4)
  - how it was derived: Coded -0.5 accuracy, 0.5 sharing.
- **Type [derived]**: binary, between participants (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Random assignment to one of two conditions.
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Assigned once per participant.

### Yes/no response (accuracy judgment or sharing intention)

`v_resp_s1`

- **Role [derived]**: dv (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Responses are the outcome used to compute discernment.
- **Type**: binary yes/no (analysed with a linear probability model, z scored) (p. 4)
- **Definition [derived]**: Accuracy: whether the claim is judged accurate. Sharing: whether the participant would consider sharing the story. (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Paraphrase of the two questions.
- **Transformation**: z scored for the main regressions. (p. 4)
- **Observation unit**: participant × headline rating (p. 4)

### Truth discernment (Study 1)

`v_discern_s1`

- **Role**: dv (p. 3)
- **Definition**: The difference in accuracy judgments (or sharing intentions) between true and false headlines; higher scores indicate higher sensitivity to truth relative to falsity. (p. 3)
- **Aggregation [derived]**: Proportion 'yes' for true headlines minus proportion 'yes' for false headlines (worked example: 9/15 true and 3/15 false gives .6 − .2 = .4); in the regressions it is the veracity coefficient. (p. 3, p. 4)
  - how it was derived: The example is the paper's; the regression reading is from the analysis passage.
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Computed from one participant's 30 responses.

### Individual-differences measures (CRT, science knowledge, MMS, partisanship, distance to epicenter)

`v_indiff`

- **Role [derived]**: moderator (p. 4, p. 5)
  - how it was derived: Interacted with veracity and condition.
- **Type**: continuous scores, z scored (p. 4)
- **Definition**: CRT score (six items, α = .69), science knowledge (17 items, α = .77), Medical Maximizer-Minimizer Scale score (α = .86), preference for the Republican over the Democratic Party, and log distance to the nearest county with at least 10 confirmed cases. (p. 3, p. 4, p. 5)
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Measured once per participant.

### COVID-19 concern and news checking

`v_covid_att`

- **Role [derived]**: covariate (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Used in an exploratory correlational analysis.
- **Type**: concern: 0-100 slider; news checking: 1-5 (p. 3)
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant (p. 3)
  - how it was derived: Answered once per participant.

## Analysis

### Study 1 main analysis: veracity × condition

`a_s1_main`

- **Aim**: Compare discernment between the accuracy and sharing conditions. (p. 4)
- **Inputs**: z-scored yes/no ratings; veracity coded -0.5/0.5; condition coded -0.5 (accuracy) / 0.5 (sharing). (p. 4)
- **Method**: Linear regression (linear probability model) at the level of the rating, with robust standard errors clustered on participants and headline. (p. 4, p. 10)
- **Criterion**: The coefficient on veracity indicates overall discernment; the veracity × condition interaction indicates how much discernment differs between conditions. (p. 4)
- **Robustness**: Logistic regression gave qualitatively equivalent results; results across attentiveness levels and headline exclusions are in Supplement Tables S2 and S3; a crossed-random-effects model is in Table S4. (p. 4, p. 3, p. 5)

### Study 1 individual differences and discernment

`a_s1_indiff`

- **Aim**: Test whether CRT score, science knowledge, partisanship, distance to the nearest epicenter and MMS score interact with veracity (discernment) and with veracity × condition. (p. 2, p. 4)
- **Method [derived]**: The same rating-level regression with each z-scored measure and its interactions; simple effects within each condition × veracity cell (Table 1); pairwise correlations and models for COVID-19 concern and news checking (Table 2). (p. 4, p. 5, p. 6)
  - how it was derived: Combined from the Results passages and table captions.
- **Robustness**: All relationships were robust to controls for age, gender, education, ethnicity and their interactions with veracity and condition. (p. 4)

## Reported results

### Study 1: discernment higher for accuracy than sharing

`res_s1_main`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 4)
- **Interaction**: β = −0.126, F(1, 25586) = 42.24, p < .0001 (veracity × condition). (p. 4)
- **Simple effects of veracity**: Accuracy condition: Cohen's d = 0.657, 95% CI [0.477, 0.836], F(1, 25586) = 42.24, p < .0001. Sharing condition: d = 0.121, 95% CI [0.030, 0.212], F(1, 25586) = 6.74, p = .009. (p. 4)
- **Descriptives**: For false headlines, 32.4% more people were willing to share the headlines than rated them as accurate. (p. 4)

### Study 1: CRT and discernment

`res_s1_crt`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 4)
- **Tests**: CRT × veracity: accuracy F(1, 25582) = 34.95, p < .0001; sharing F(1, 25582) = 4.98, p = .026; three-way with condition F(1, 25582) = 14.68, p = .0001. (p. 4)
- **Direction**: CRT negatively correlated with belief in false headlines and uncorrelated with belief in true headlines; negatively correlated with sharing of both types, more so for false headlines. (p. 5)

### Study 1: science knowledge and discernment

`res_s1_sci`

- **Result kind [implementation]**: reported (p. 5)
- **Tests**: Science knowledge × veracity: accuracy F(1, 25552) = 32.80, p < .0001; sharing F(1, 25552) = 10.02, p = .002; three-way with condition F(1, 25552) = 7.59, p = .006. (p. 5)
- **Direction**: Negatively correlated with belief in false headlines and positively with belief in true headlines; negatively correlated with sharing of false headlines and uncorrelated with sharing of true headlines. (p. 5)

## Simulation scope

### Study 1 simulation scope

`sim_s1`

- **Steps [implementation]**: Replay the news-evaluation / news-sharing task for 30 headlines in random order with the condition's question. The COVID-19 questions, CRT, science quiz, MMS, demographics and screeners are not replayed (their wording is not in the paper). (p. 3)
- **Roles [implementation]**: Every participant is an AI participant; no scripted roles. (p. 3)
- **Conditions [implementation]**: Accuracy and sharing, both runnable; random assignment is replaced by an exact number of AI participants per condition. (p. 3)
- **Outcomes [implementation]**: Yes/no response per headline, discernment, and the veracity × condition interaction. Individual-differences correlates cannot be tested. (p. 4)
- **Limits [implementation]**: Headlines appear as text only (pictures and lede sentences are on OSF); all 30 headlines are shown in one prompt in presentation order; no demographic persona is assigned; sharing is hypothetical; AI agreement with the paper would not show that people behave the same way. (p. 3, p. 9)
