# Paper context

## Overview

`ov`

- **Year** (verbatim, p. 1):
  > 2018
- **Method type**: Two online between/within-participant rating experiments (Amazon Mechanical Turk) alongside a Rational Speech Act computational model of negated gradable adjectives. (p. 2, p. 4)
- **Summary**: Tests whether listeners' uncertainty about the meaning of negation markers (un-, not) predicts how morphological antonyms (unhappy), negated positives (not happy), negated antonyms (not unhappy) and positives (happy) are ordered on a scale, depending on whether the adjectives are heard alone or together in one context. (p. 2)

## Research question and theory

`bg`

- **Question**: If "Jones is not unhappy", does that mean that Jones is happy? How does negation give rise to a multiplicity of meanings (unhappy, not happy, not unhappy)? (p. 2)
- **Gap**: No computational account of these interpretations had been tested against human behavioral data. (p. 2)
- **Theory**: Rational Speech Act model of gradable adjectives with lexical uncertainty: listeners are uncertain whether negation markers express contradictory or contrary opposition. (p. 2)
- **Concepts**: Contrary opposition: both predicates cannot be true at once but both can be false (tall/short). Contradictory opposition: falsity of one entails truth of the other. (p. 2)

## Findings

`find`

- **Answer**: Morphological antonyms and negated positives are not immediately differentiated, except when both are present in the same context; lexical antonyms are distinguished from negated positives regardless. (p. 7)
- **Support**: Interaction of antonym type with the antonym vs. negated-positive contrast (Expt. 1 and Expt. 2) and of context with that contrast for morphological antonyms (Expt. 2). (p. 4, p. 6)
- **Mechanism proposed**: Uncertainty about whether negation markers express contradiction or contrary opposition, resolved by joint reasoning over speaker lexicon and utterance alternatives. (p. 2, p. 7)

## Validity and openness

`lim`

- **Limitations**: The authors stipulate, rather than derive, differences in meaning for morphological vs. lexical antonym pairs. (p. 7)
- **Replications**: Expt. 2 is a larger, preregistered replication of the exploratory Expt. 1. (p. 4)
- **Openness [to check]**: Paper states paradigms, models, analysis scripts and data are available at https://mhtess.github.io (not consulted; no external research authorized). Expt. 2 preregistered at osf.io/p7f25/. (p. 7, p. 5)
- **Scope**: Model prior over lexica assumed uniform; frequency as a proxy for the prior is suggested as a further test. (p. 7)
