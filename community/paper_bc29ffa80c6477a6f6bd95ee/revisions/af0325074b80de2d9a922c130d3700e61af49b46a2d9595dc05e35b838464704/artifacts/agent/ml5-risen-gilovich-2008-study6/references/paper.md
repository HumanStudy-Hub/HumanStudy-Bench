# Paper context

## Overview

`ov`

- **Year**: 2020 (p. 1)
- **Method type**: Registered, preregistered multisite direct replication; between-subjects factorial experiment (p. 3, p. 2)
- **Summary**: Replicates Study 6 of Risen & Gilovich (2008): whether imagining having tempted fate raises the judged likelihood of an ironic bad outcome (being called on in class), and whether this is magnified by cognitive load. Run with an updated protocol at similar universities, at dissimilar universities, and on MTurk under the earlier RP:P protocol. (p. 1, p. 2)

## Research question and gap

`bg`

- **Question**: Do people believe that tempting fate is punished with ironic bad outcomes, and is that belief stronger under cognitive load? (p. 1)
- **Gap**: A previous MTurk replication (Frank & Mathur, 2016) failed to replicate both effects; the original authors had worried that cognitive load works less well online and that the scenario is less salient outside an elite university. Many Labs 2 replicated the main effect (smaller) but did not assess the interaction. (p. 2, p. 3)
- **Theory**: Deliberative (System 2) thinking may suppress irrational tempting-fate heuristics, so cognitive load should magnify the effect. (p. 2)
