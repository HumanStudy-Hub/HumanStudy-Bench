# Paper context

## Overview

`ov`

Many Labs replication of 13 effects across 36 samples

- **Year**: 2014 (p. 1)
- **Method type**: Multi-site direct replication of 13 effects, bundled into one standardized online-administered session; cross-site moderation analysis (p. 2)
- **Summary**: Replicability of 13 effects tested across 36 independent samples totaling 6,344 participants; aggregate and site-level moderation by lab versus online and US versus international. (p. 1)

## Research question and gap

`bg`

- **Question**: Variation in replicability of 13 classic and contemporary psychological effects across 36 samples and settings (p. 2)
- **Gap**: Little attempt has been made to assess the variation in replicability of findings across samples and research contexts. (p. 2)
- **Contribution**: Establish a paradigm for testing replicability across samples and settings and provide a rich data set (p. 2)

## Main conclusion

`fd`

- **Answer**: To predict effect size it is much more important to know what effect is being studied than to know the sample or setting. (p. 10)
- **Support**: Variation attributable to effect (ICC .75) rather than sample (ICC .005); moderation sporadic and small (p. 8, p. 9)

## Validity and openness

`lim`

- **Scope**: Small, nonrandom sample of studies; most societies sampled were relatively WEIRD (p. 10)
- **Openness**: All materials, data, videos of the procedure and the original preregistered design are available at https://osf.io/ydpbf/ (p. 10)
