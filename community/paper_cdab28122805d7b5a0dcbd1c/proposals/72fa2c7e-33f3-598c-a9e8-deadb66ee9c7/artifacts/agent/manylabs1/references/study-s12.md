# Study 12: Implicit math attitudes (sex differences and implicit-explicit relation)

`s12`

Sex differences in implicit math attitudes (Effect 12) and implicit-explicit math attitude relation (Effect 13) (Nosek, Banaji, & Greenwald, 2002)

## Participants

### Implicit math attitudes: participants analysed

`s12_pt`

- **Sex composition**: 4273 reported female, 2060 male, 11 did not report (p. 8)
- **N in the analysis (Table 2)**: 5,842 (sex difference); 5,623 (implicit-explicit correlation) (p. 7)
- **Exclusions**: Exclusions for IAT error rates and missing responses stated in the supplement; one sample removed for a systemic reaction-time recording error (p. 9, p. 7)

## Design

### Implicit math attitudes: design

`s12_dz`

- **Design type**: Single IAT plus explicit attitude items, always the final study; effect 12 compares men and women (participant sex is a measured characteristic, not an assigned condition); effect 13 is correlational (p. 5, p. 6)
- **Assignment [derived]**: No manipulated condition; sex is a participant characteristic (p. 8)
  - how it was derived: Sex groups are reported as self-reported gender counts, not assigned

## Procedure

Order of steps:

- Complete the math-versus-arts IAT (always last) (participant sees: Implicit math attitudes: participant materials) (p. 5, p. 6)
- Answer the explicit math attitude items (participant sees: Implicit math attitudes: participant materials) (p. 5)

### Implicit math attitudes: procedure

`s12_pc`

- **Task**: Complete one IAT (D algorithm scoring), then the explicit math attitude items (p. 5, p. 9)
- **Changes in the replication**: Simplified from four IATs to a single IAT for length (p. 5)

## Variables

### Implicit math attitudes: participant sex

`s12_viv`

- **Role [derived]**: iv (grouping) (p. 9)
  - how it was derived: Participant sex is the grouping variable in the effect 12 test
- **Type**: categorical (men, women) (p. 8)
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant
  - how it was derived: One record per participant

### Implicit math attitudes: IAT D score

`s12_vim`

- **Role [derived]**: dv (effect 12); iv (effect 13) (p. 9)
  - how it was derived: Outcome in the sex-difference test and in the correlation, per the supplement
- **Type**: continuous D score (p. 9)
- **Scoring**: D algorithm; latencies < 400 ms and > 10,000 ms removed; trial latencies from trial start until a correct response; >40% errors on a single block or >30% overall excluded (p. 9)
- **Observation unit [derived]**: participant
  - how it was derived: One D score per participant

## Simulation scope

### Simulation scope: implicit math attitudes

`sim_iat`

- **Steps [implementation]**: Not runnable as published by a text participant.
- **Roles [implementation]**: Participant only.
- **Conditions [implementation]**: None assigned; sex is a measured characteristic.
- **Outcomes [implementation]**: IAT D score needs reaction times, which text output cannot provide.
- **Limits [implementation]**: Excluded from AI runs unless the researcher approves a labelled adaptation.

## Open issues

- **The IAT records response latencies, which a text-only AI participant cannot produce** [check, `i_s12_iat_text`, s12]: The IAT is scored from trial-by-trial reaction times (D algorithm); a text model answering in prose has no latencies. Suggested: Decide whether to exclude this study from AI runs or design a labelled adaptation. (p. 9)
