# Paper context

## Paper overview

`n-overview`

- **Method type [to check]**: Three pre-registered online quasi-experimental within-subjects variations of the belief 'update method', analysed on trials the participant rated as valence-neutral. (p. 2, p. 6)
- **Summary [to check]**: Tests whether asymmetric belief updating (greater updating when the base rate exceeds vs. falls short of the initial estimate) appears with neutral life events, where optimism cannot explain it. Reports a significant asymmetry in all three studies and unstable results across samples and analytic techniques. (p. 2)
- **Year**: Not stated in the paper.

## Question and background

`n-background`

- **Research question [to check]**: Can asymmetric belief updating be observed with valence-neutral life events, where a motivational (optimism) account cannot apply? (p. 5, p. 9)
- **Gap [to check]**: Evidence for optimistic belief updating comes from the update method, which may produce asymmetries as a statistical artefact; prior work used negative (and some positive) events, never neutral events as the focal stimuli. (p. 3, p. 5)
- **Theory [to check]**: Statistical artefact hypothesis: on the bounded 0-100 scale, BR error, individuating knowledge and normatively required belief change cannot be equated across desirable and undesirable trials, so even Bayesian agents show apparent optimism (Shah et al., 2016). (p. 4)
- **Key concepts [to check]**: Update method (E1, BR, E2); direction of error (BR > E1 upwards, otherwise downwards); update value; event valence; optimistic belief updating as 'a valence-dependent asymmetry in how people use favorable and unfavorable information'. (p. 3, p. 4, p. 8)

## Findings

`f-main`

- **Answer [to check]**: An asymmetry in belief updating appears with neutral events in all three studies, so it cannot be attributed to optimism; its size and significance vary across samples and analytic techniques. (p. 13, p. 14)
- **Contribution [to check]**: Presence of an asymmetry with valenced events is insufficient evidence for a motivational bias; the update method is called unfit for purpose. (p. 14, p. 15)
- **Proposed mechanism [to check]**: Variability in individuating information, estimation error and base rate error across samples (SI Figs. S3-S5) may produce the differing results. (p. 14)

## Limitations and openness

`l-validity`

- **Limitations [to check]**: No control for familiarity, vividness, emotional arousal or perceived controllability; only one framing (likelihood of experiencing, not of not experiencing). Quasi-experimental: neutral trials are self-classified after the BR. (p. 15, p. 5)
- **Replications [to check]**: Study 4 (20 of the 51 events) is pre-registered and reported only in the SI. (p. 13)
- **Openness [to check]**: Datasets and code on an OSF project page; no URL printed in the paper; pre-registration exists on OSF. (p. 16)
- **Ethics [to check]**: Approved by the Birkbeck Department of Psychological Sciences Research Ethics Committee (ref 161755); informed consent. (p. 16)
