# Many Labs 2, Slate 2 (15 effects, 61 samples)

`slate2`

One survey script administering the fifteen Slate 2 effects in random order, followed by individual difference measures, data quality items and demographics.

## Participants

### Participants, Slate 2

`p-slate2`

- **Setting** (verbatim, p. 16):
  > The study was administered over the Internet for standardization across locations. At some locations, participants completed the survey in a lab or room on computers or tablets, whereas in other locations the participants completed the survey entirely online at their own convenience. Surveys were created in Qualtrics software (qualtrics.com) and unique links to run the studies were sent to each data collection team to track the origin of data.
- **Recruitment** (verbatim, p. 15):
  > An open invitation to participate as a data collection site in Many Labs 2 was issued in early 2014.
- **Eligibility of sites**: To be eligible, participating labs agreed to administer their assigned study procedure to at least 80 participants and to collect as many as was feasible (p. 15)
- **Characteristics** (verbatim, p. 15, p. 16):
  > For Slate 2, 35.9% were men, 62.9% were women, 0.4% selected “Other”, and 0.8% selected “Prefer not to answer”.
- **Number of samples**: 61 samples (of 125 samples in total, 15,305 participants in total across both slates) (p. 15)
- **Unit of analysis [derived]**: The participant within a data collection site (sample); the paper pools all samples and also analyses samples separately (p. 15)
  - how it was derived: The paper reports pooled participant-level tests and per-sample effect sizes.
- **Roles [derived]**: All participants are survey respondents; the paper describes no confederates, experimenters or interaction partners in the survey (p. 16)
  - how it was derived: The procedure is a single-participant online survey.
- **Participants in this slate**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Compensation**: Not stated in the paper.

## Design

### Design, Slate 2

`d-slate2`

- **Design type**: Each effect is a two-condition between-participants experiment or a simple correlation; all effects of the slate are run in one session by every participant (p. 9, p. 16)
- **Order** (verbatim, p. 17):
  > fully randomized order at the level of participants, followed by the individual difference measures in randomized order, and then closing with demographics measures and debriefing.
- **Assignment to condition**: Not stated in the paper.

## Procedure

Order of steps:

- Informed consent (p. 16)
- Effects of the slate in random order per participant (p. 16, p. 17)
  - **Order rule**: Effects are presented in a fully randomized order at the level of participants (p. 17)
  - Effect 14: False consensus: traffic ticket (participant sees: False consensus: traffic ticket: printed items) (p. 40)
  - Effect 15: Vertical position and power (participant sees: Vertical position and power, Long line connecting manager to team (7 cm): printed items; Vertical position and power, Short line connecting manager to team (2 cm): printed items) (p. 40, p. 41)
  - Effect 16: Framing decisions (participant sees: Framing decisions, Cheap item on sale at the other branch (vase $250, wall hanging $30 reduced to $20): printed items; Framing decisions, Costly item on sale at the other branch (vase $30, wall hanging $250 reduced to $240): printed items) (p. 41, p. 42)
  - Effect 17: Trolley dilemma 2 (participant sees: Trolley dilemma 2, Ned scenario: printed items; Trolley dilemma 2, Oscar scenario: printed items) (p. 42)
  - Effect 18: Tempting fate (participant sees: Tempting fate, Tempting fate (has not done the reading): printed items; Tempting fate, Not tempting fate (has done the reading): printed items) (p. 43, p. 44)
  - Effect 19: Actions are choices? (participant sees: Actions are choices?, Recall personal actions: printed items; Actions are choices?, Recall interpersonal actions: printed items) (p. 44, p. 45)
