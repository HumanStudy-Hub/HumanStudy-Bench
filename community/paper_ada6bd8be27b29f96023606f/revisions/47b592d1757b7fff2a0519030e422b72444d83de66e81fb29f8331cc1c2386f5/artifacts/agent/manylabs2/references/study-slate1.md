# Many Labs 2, Slate 1 (13 effects, 64 samples)

`slate1`

One survey script administering the thirteen Slate 1 effects in random order, followed by individual difference measures, data quality items and demographics.

## Hypotheses

### Hypothesis: Direction and socioeconomic status

`h-huan`

- **Statement**: People place a high-SES person further toward the cardinal direction where wealth is concentrated in their own experience: U.S. participants expect the high-SES person to live further north than the low-SES person, Hong Kong participants the reverse. (p. 22, p. 23)

### Hypothesis: Structure and goal pursuit

`h-kay`

- **Statement**: Willingness to pursue goals is higher after exposure to structured versus random events. (p. 25)

### Hypothesis: Disfluency engages analytical processing

`h-alt`

- **Statement**: Syllogism performance is higher when the font is harder to read versus easier to read (incidental disfluency activates analytic processing). (p. 26, p. 27)

### Hypothesis: Moral foundations

`h-grah`

- **Statement**: "Binding" foundations (ingroup, authority, purity) are perceived as more morally relevant by members of the political right than the political left. (p. 28)

### Hypothesis: Affect and risk

`h-rott`

- **Statement**: Affectively attractive choices are more preferred when they are uncertain versus definite. (p. 29)

### Hypothesis: Priming consumerism

`h-bau`

- **Statement**: Trust is lower when thinking of others as consumers versus thinking of others as individuals. (p. 30)

### Hypothesis: Correspondence bias

`h-miya`

- **Statement**: Perceivers ascribe to an actor an attitude corresponding to the actor's assigned behaviour (correspondence bias), more strongly among Americans than Japanese. (p. 31, p. 32)

### Hypothesis: Disgust predicts homophobia

`h-inba`

- **Statement**: Disgust sensitivity and judged intentionality are more strongly related when the director encourages gay kissing than kissing. (p. 33)

### Hypothesis: Incidental anchors

`h-crit`

- **Statement**: Sales estimates are influenced by incidental anchors in the environment (a high versus low number in the product name). (p. 34, p. 35)

### Hypothesis: Social value orientations

`h-van`

- **Statement**: More siblings is positively related with prosocial orientation (sibling-prosocial hypothesis). (p. 36)

### Hypothesis: Trolley dilemma 1

`h-haus12`

- **Statement**: Harm is judged more permissible as a foreseen side effect than as the means to a greater good (principle of double effect). (p. 37)

### Hypothesis: Sociometric status and well-being

`h-and`

- **Statement**: Subjective well-being is higher for participants exposed to higher versus lower sociometric status descriptions. (p. 38)

### Hypothesis: False consensus: supermarket

`h-ross1`

- **Statement**: Participants' choices are positively related to their estimate of the percentage of others who would make the same choice (supermarket vignette). (p. 39)

## Participants

### Participants, Slate 1

`p-slate1`

- **Setting** (verbatim, p. 16):
  > The study was administered over the Internet for standardization across locations. At some locations, participants completed the survey in a lab or room on computers or tablets, whereas in other locations the participants completed the survey entirely online at their own convenience. Surveys were created in Qualtrics software (qualtrics.com) and unique links to run the studies were sent to each data collection team to track the origin of data.
- **Recruitment** (verbatim, p. 15):
  > An open invitation to participate as a data collection site in Many Labs 2 was issued in early 2014.
- **Eligibility of sites**: To be eligible, participating labs agreed to administer their assigned study procedure to at least 80 participants and to collect as many as was feasible (p. 15)
- **Characteristics** (verbatim, p. 15):
  > Of those that responded to demographics questions, in Slate 1 34.5% were men, 64.4% were women, 0.3% selected “Other”, and 0.8% selected “Prefer not to answer”. The average age for Slate 1 was 22.37 (SD = 7.09)
- **Number of samples**: 64 samples (of 125 samples in total, 15,305 participants in total across both slates) (p. 15)
- **Unit of analysis [derived]**: The participant within a data collection site (sample); the paper pools all samples and also analyses samples separately (p. 15)
  - how it was derived: The paper reports pooled participant-level tests and per-sample effect sizes.
- **Roles [derived]**: All participants are survey respondents; the paper describes no confederates, experimenters or interaction partners in the survey (p. 16)
  - how it was derived: The procedure is a single-participant online survey.
- **Participants in this slate**: Not stated in the paper.
- **Compensation**: Not stated in the paper.

## Design

### Design, Slate 1

`d-slate1`

- **Design type**: Each effect is a two-condition between-participants experiment or a simple correlation; all effects of the slate are run in one session by every participant (p. 9, p. 16)
- **Order** (verbatim, p. 17):
  > fully randomized order at the level of participants, followed by the individual difference measures in randomized order, and then closing with demographics measures and debriefing.
