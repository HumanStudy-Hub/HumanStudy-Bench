# Paper context

## Overview of Many Labs 2

`n-overview`

- **Method type [derived]**: Preregistered, peer-reviewed-in-advance multi-site replications of 28 published findings, each administered by an online survey (Qualtrics) in lab or online settings across many samples (p. 5, p. 11)
  - how it was derived: Combines the abstract and the Registered Report statement.
- **Summary** (verbatim, p. 5):
  > We conducted preregistered replications of 28 classic and contemporary published findings with protocols that were peer reviewed in advance to examine variation in effect magnitudes across sample and setting. Each protocol was administered to approximately half of 125 samples and 15,305 total participants from 36 countries and territories.

## Research question: person, situation or sampling error?

`n-background`

- **Question** (verbatim, p. 6):
  > The present research contributes evidence toward understanding the contribution of variation in sample and setting for observing psychological effects.
- **Gap** (verbatim, p. 8):
  > A limitation of the first “Many Labs” is that there was a small number of effects and there was no reason to presume them to vary substantially across sample and setting. It is possible that those effects are more robust and homogenous than the typical behavioral phenomena, or that the populations were more homogenous than initially expected.
- **Concepts**: Variation in effect magnitudes across sample and setting versus sampling error; WEIRD versus less WEIRD cultures; lab versus online administration; task order (p. 6, p. 5)

## Replications and variation

`f-main`

- **Answer** (verbatim, p. 5):
  > Cumulatively, variability in observed effect sizes was more attributable to the effect being studied than the sample or setting in which it was studied.
- **Support** (verbatim, p. 69):
  > Using conventional statistical significance (p < 0.05), fifteen (54%) of the replications provided evidence in the same direction and statistically significant consistent with the original finding. With a strict significance criterion (p < 0.0001), fourteen (50%) provide such evidence reflecting the extremely high powered design (for Inbar et al., 2009 the replication p-value was 0.02).
- **Decline in effect sizes** (verbatim, p. 69):
  > In WEIRD samples, the median Cohen’s d effect size for original findings was 0.60 and for replications was 0.15 indicating a substantial decline (Open Science Collaboration, 2015).

## Scope and validity limits
